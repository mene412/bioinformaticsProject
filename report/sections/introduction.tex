\section{Introduction}
\paragraph{What is metagenomics} Metagenomics is the application of modern genmic techniques to the study of communities of microbial organisms directly in their natural environment. This approach bypasses the need for isolating and cultivating individual species in the lab, which is particularly beneficial since many bacteria and viruses cannot survive in isolation.

In principle is possible to access 100\% of the genetic resources of an environment.

The applications are many:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Plant-microbe interaction for improving crop yield
    \item Monitoring and optimizing wastewater treatment
    \item Bioprospecting: hunting for novel enzymes and compounds
    \item Function and ecology of the oceans
    \item Development of functional feed
    \item Monitoring pathogens
    \item Monitoring polution
    \item Disease surveillance and treatment
\end{itemize}

The metagenomics workflow (\cite{Metagenomics_guide}): experimental design, sampling, DNA extraction and amplification, DNA sequencing, assembling and binning, statistical analysis and data managment.

Sampling and Processing: Obtain representative, high-quality DNA from environmental samples; involves DNA extraction. Specific protocols are often needed for different sample types to ensure sufficient DNA amounts and minimal host DNA contamination.

Sequencing Technology: Utilizing NGS platforms (454/Roche, Illumina/Solexa) to generate sequence data from DNA. These technologies offer varying read lengths, costs, and throughputs compared to Sanger sequencing.

Assembly: Combine short reads into longer contigs, aiming for genomes or full-length genes. This can be done via reference-based or de novo assembly, with specific tools designed to handle the non-clonality of natural populations.

Binning: Group DNA sequences into bins, potentially representing individual or related genomes. Binning algorithms use compositional features or similarity to known genes, and their reliability can depend on read length and available reference data.

Annotation: Predict genes and assign their functions/taxonomic classification based on databases. This involves feature prediction to identify genes and functional annotation by comparing sequences to existing gene or protein libraries.

Experimental Design and Statistical Analysis: Plan experiments with proper replication; apply statistical tools for data analysis. This ensures that observed patterns are statistically significant and distinguishes between biological and technical variation.

Data Storage and Sharing: Manage and share metagenomic data using centralized services and standard formats. This is crucial for comparing individual projects and utilizing rich information from other metagenome projects, with initiatives like the Genomic Standards Consortium (GSC) facilitating coordination
